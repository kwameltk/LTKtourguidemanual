Core Operations

🚐 Pick-Ups

Pick-ups are your first impression. Every location requires a slightly different approach — know your context before the client arrives.

From Nkawkaw

Picking Up from Nkawkaw

Nkawkaw is the business heart of Kwahu — the lowland gateway where trade began, anchored by a historic train station connecting Kumasi, Koforidua, and Accra. Use this journey to differentiate the low ridge from the mountain plateau above.

💡

Explain that Nkawkaw was originally a smaller settlement below Obomeng — parents descending the mountain to farm gradually turned it into a commercial hub. Cocoa was the key cash crop that started it all.

  • Introduce Nkawkaw as the earliest Kwahu settlement and trade hub
  • Explain the train station's historic role connecting major Ghanaian cities
  • Highlight the contrast between the lowland business district and the mountain top
  • Use the journey up the mountain as natural storytelling — the plateau surprise is powerful
From Accra

Picking Up from Accra

A long corridor — roughly 2.5 to 3 hours — that passes through Ghana's agricultural heartland. Begin interpretation from the moment the vehicle leaves Accra.

💡

The Accra–Kwahu drive is not dead time. It is the opening chapter of the experience. Use it deliberately.

  • Introduce yourself and communicate the full travel time and possible traffic variations
  • Name key landmarks: Nsawam, Suhum, Bunsu, Akyem area, Oframase, then Nkawkaw
  • Highlight the Eastern Corridor — Koforidua as capital, cocoa farming, palm plantations
  • Mention potential detour attractions: Aboretuom Eco Park, Umbrella Rock, Three-Headed Palm Tree, Koforidua Beads Market
  • Build anticipation for the mountain ascent before reaching Nkawkaw
From Hotel / Homestay

Picking Up from Hotel or Homestay

Mostly for domestic tourists already lodged in Kwahu. You may join their vehicle — either with your own seat or sharing — and your job from the first moment is to make the day feel welcoming.

  • Introduce yourself and LTK Tours clearly and warmly
  • Communicate the day's itinerary — note it is often flexible, so describe the spirit rather than a fixed schedule
  • Be high energy: this sets the tone for the day
  • Open car doors for tourists where possible — small gestures build early rapport
  • Mention any community activity happening that day (funerals, festivals) so tourists understand what they may encounter
🤝

Friendliness from the start opens the door for open conversation and tips later in the day.

From Mpraeso

Picking Up from Mpraeso (Designated Point)

This requires the highest level of time discipline. The tour guide must arrive before the tourist — always. Everything counts from the first second: your appearance, your posture, your greeting.

  • Arrive at the pickup point before the tourist — no exceptions
  • Introduce yourself and give an itinerary update
  • Ask how they are doing; gauge their energy level
  • If hiking is planned: "Are you excited? Have you done hiking like this before?"
  • Maintain eye contact throughout — it builds trust for the whole day
Remember: Tourists write reviews based on every interaction — from first look to final goodbye. Your punctuality, demeanor, and attitude are already part of the score.

👋 Departures

How you say goodbye matters as much as how you say hello. A clean, professional departure protects the company, the tourist's experience, and your own reputation.

Hotel Departure

Departing from Hotel

  • Create a proper goodbye moment — do not rush away
  • Express genuine gratitude for the tourist choosing LTK Tours
  • Mention that a review link will be sent to them — encourage honest feedback
  • Confirm payment with the group leader before leaving — always check with Kwame (Customer Service)
  • Do not solicit tips — be natural, be warm, and tips will follow your service quality
Financial Rule: If tourists give you money, deposit it into the LTK Tours bank account immediately — same day, in town. No exceptions. Contact Customer Service via WhatsApp right after the tour. SMS if WhatsApp is unavailable.

Company Contact: 0545748380

Nkawkaw Station

Departing from Nkawkaw Station

  • Take the Atibie road — point out Atibie Government Hospital and midwifery school
  • Show the "rich people area" housing on the left side
  • Stop briefly for tourists to photograph or view the other side of Odweanoma Mountain
  • About one hour before departure, ask if tourists need food — arrange ahead where possible by sharing food details with Customer Service
  • At the station, help tourists get a bus and ensure loaders do not overcharge for bags — bargain on their behalf
  • Help tourists board and secure their luggage before saying goodbye
  • Direct tourists to toilet facilities — and let them know honestly if conditions are basic
En Route Departure

Departing En Route (Vehicle Drop-Off)

When the tourist's vehicle will drop you off along the way back to their hotel, handle payment before you exit the vehicle.

  • Remind the group leader about payment — reference the invoice already shared
  • Keep the conversation friendly — this is not a transaction moment, it is a final connection moment
  • Communicate clearly with Customer Service after your drop-off
💡

The moment you are dropped off is not the end of your professional responsibility. Confirm payment and report to Customer Service the same day.

Nsawam → Suhum → Bunso → Anyinam → Koforidua Jcn → Nkawkaw

Talk about: Cocoa farming, palm plantations, roadside fruit markets, Eastern Region as Ghana's agricultural heartland. Beads market in Koforidua. Umbrella Rock. Three-Headed Palm.

Nkawkaw → Kwahu Mountain Ascent

One of the most dramatic moments of every tour. Guests are climbing one of Ghana's highest inhabited mountain ranges — build the excitement.

  • Hairpin bends engineered into the mountainside
  • Beautiful valleys visible below
  • Thick forest vegetation closing in
  • Noticeably cooler temperatures as elevation increases
  • Panoramic viewpoints overlooking Nkawkaw town
🏔

This is when to explain why Kwahu is called "The Mountain Kingdom of Ghana." Encourage guests to appreciate the engineering behind the road construction.

Key Routes

LTK Tour Routes

Mpraeso to Twenedurase

This route introduces visitors to the history, people, economic activities, architecture, traditional beliefs, Easter culture, and everyday life of the Kwahu people.

1. Mpraeso: Starting the Kwahu Experience

The journey begins from Mpraeso, one of the principal towns within Kwahu South Municipality. Mpraeso is situated on the Kwahu hills and serves as an important administrative and commercial centre within the area.

The guide should explain that Mpraeso is not simply a town through which tourists are passing. It is an important centre for government administration, commerce, education, transportation, and everyday life on the Kwahu Plateau.

According to the local account used for this LTK Tours route interpretation, the people associated with Mpraeso are connected to the Asona clan, whose traditional totem is the crow. The guide should introduce this as part of the clan identity of the community and use it as an opportunity to explain the broader Akan clan system to visitors.

2. The Circuit Court

As you leave Mpraeso heading towards Twenedurase, one of the first notable institutions visitors may observe is the Circuit Court.

The court represents the formal legal system operating within the community. Its role is connected to maintaining law and order and dealing with legal matters within its jurisdiction. This is a good opportunity to explain the coexistence of traditional authority and modern government institutions in Kwahu.

3. The Banana Processing Factory and Kwahu's Agricultural Potential

Further along the route, visitors may notice a banana processing facility established during the Akufo-Addo administration as part of an initiative intended to support employment and economic activity. The project was associated with YouStart/Enable Youth-related development initiatives.

Kwahu and its surrounding communities have significant agricultural activity, including banana cultivation. Communities around Bepong and its surrounding villages have been identified locally as important suppliers of bananas. The intended production included products such as banana chips.

Production has not operated consistently since the project's establishment around 2020, although there have been more recent signs of agricultural development and banana planting around the facility.

“What you are seeing here represents both the potential and the unfinished story of agricultural industrialisation in Kwahu. The raw materials are available, the farmers are here, and the market exists. The opportunity is to connect production with processing so that more of the economic value remains within the community.”

4. Kwahu South Municipal Assembly

Further along the route, the guide can point out the Kwahu South Municipal Assembly, which serves as the local government administrative centre. Ghana operates a decentralised local government system in which Municipal and District Assemblies coordinate development at the local level.

5. Morningside Hill

As the vehicle continues, draw attention to Morningside Hill, where a prominent mansion can be seen. According to the local story incorporated into this route, the property is associated with a person from Obomeng and has historically been used as a place where political executives and government officials stayed during visits to Kwahu.

6. Obomeng: The Heart of the Easter Experience

As the journey approaches Obomeng, the atmosphere and architecture begin to tell another important part of the Kwahu story. Easter has developed into one of the most important periods for social gatherings, entertainment, tourism, family reunions, and economic activity throughout Kwahu.

The Kwahu Easter tradition has connections to the history of Christianity and the Presbyterian/Basel missionary movement in Kwahu. The missionary Fritz Ramseyer is particularly important in the history of Presbyterian missionary work in the area. Today, Easter attracts people from across Ghana and members of the Kwahu diaspora.

Easter represents homecoming, family, religion, culture, tourism, business, entertainment, and community identity.

7. The Krontihene's House and 8. The Atia Yaw Statue

When approaching the Krontihene's house, slow down so visitors can observe the traditional setting. Explain customary leadership, royal lineage, the stool system, and the importance of traditional institutions in Kwahu society.

Opposite the house, visitors can see the Atia Yaw statue. According to local tradition, Atia Yaw served as an important traditional priest, or Okomfo. The statue reminds visitors that Christianity did not arrive in an empty cultural space: Kwahu already had spiritual institutions, priests, beliefs, sacred places, and community structures.

9. Kwahu's Roads and Traditional Geography

Use the road leading towards Nkoko to explain that Kwahu has historically had different routes leading into and away from the plateau. One route leads into Obomeng, traditionally described locally as comparable to the “hall” or central receiving space of the wider Kwahu area. Another route provides an exit towards Atibie.

10. The Kwame Nkrumah Water Tower

The tall tower represents an interesting chapter in the history of infrastructure development in Kwahu. It was designed as part of a water distribution system, using elevation and gravity to distribute water. The system is no longer used in the same way today, providing an opportunity to discuss maintenance, continuity, management, and long-term planning.

11. Obomeng “Hollywood”

Introduce the two impressive buildings humorously: “Welcome to Hollywood!” Then explain that they are associated with Kwahu people who have travelled and established themselves abroad, particularly in the United States. The architecture reflects the Kwahu culture of building back home.

12. Traditional Cemeteries as Community Boundaries

Before modern road signs, GPS technology, and formal boundary markers, physical landmarks helped people understand where one community ended and another began. Cemeteries, normally located towards the outer part of a settlement, could serve this function.

13. Obo: The Architecture of Kwahu Success

Encourage tourists to look carefully at the large, modern and expensive-looking houses. Many Kwahus live in Accra, other parts of Ghana, Europe, North America, and elsewhere, but maintain strong connections to their hometowns through impressive family homes.

These homes may be most active during Easter, funerals, family gatherings, festivals, akwasidaye, and other important family occasions. Some are maintained by caretakers because their owners live and work elsewhere.

14. Nyarkoaba Nyako Hotel and Governor's Close

When passing Nyarkoaba Nyako Hotel, direct attention towards the driveway associated with Governor's Close and slow down to allow visitors to observe the main building. This reinforces the story of Kwahu entrepreneurship and investment.

15–22. The Kwahu Entrepreneurial Identity

The large homes, businesses, agricultural activities, government buildings, hotels, churches, traditional institutions, and community infrastructure all form part of the wider Kwahu story. Kwahus have developed a reputation for business, entrepreneurship, education, networking, and investment.

This identity is about mobility and reinvestment. Success may be expressed through building a family house, supporting relatives, paying school fees, supporting community projects, participating in festivals, attending funerals, establishing businesses, and supporting churches and traditional institutions.

“When you see these beautiful houses, don't only look at them as buildings. They tell a story about migration, entrepreneurship, family, success and the connection Kwahus maintain with home.”

By the time the tour reaches Twenedurase, visitors should understand who the Kwahu people are, how the communities are connected, the importance of traditional authority, Christianity, entrepreneurship, agriculture, migration, diaspora investment, Easter, homecoming, and the relationship between communities and tourism.

Mpraeso to Tafo

This route introduces visitors to the history, traditional leadership, spirituality, agriculture, forestry, architecture, and changing livelihoods of the Kwahu people.

1. Mpraeso

Mpraeso is the capital of the Kwahu South Municipality and an important commercial and administrative centre. Tourists can learn about major banking activities, the main market, the Circuit Court, and other municipal activities. The market is currently under construction, but visitors can still experience traditional market activity on Mondays through the temporary market-day arrangement.

Mpraeso belongs to the Asona Clan. Current Chief: Nana Wiafe Daduam.

2. Empaiso Palace

Stop in front of the palace and ask tourists to look carefully at the crow, the identifiable totem associated with the town. Traditional symbols and totems connect communities to ancestral identity, history, and traditional beliefs.

3. Sitan's Rock / Bonsambo

The small altar known as Sitan's Rock is associated with Bonsambo and is traditionally connected with averting or addressing curses, pouring libation, traditional spiritual activities, and community observances.

Libation is a form of communication and acknowledgement involving the spiritual world and ancestors. Do not encourage tourists to touch, enter, or perform rituals at the altar without permission.

4–5. Bepong and the Kwahu Traditional Council

After passing the two washing bays, point out the Kwahu Traditional Council. It plays an important role in Kwahu traditional affairs and customary leadership. The council is headed by a president, who also serves as the Abene Chief. Abene is the Kwahu paramountcy and traditional head of the wider Kwahu traditional area.

6. Bepong Coffee Plantation

Coffee was formerly an important source of employment in the area and was associated with CMB. There are currently approximately 300–400 acres of coffee farms. The beans are harvested and transported elsewhere for processing. Kwahu grows coffee, but it is not currently processed locally. Visitors interested in agriculture may be taken on a coffee plantation tour, subject to access and local arrangements.

7. Banana Industrial Development

Introduce the planned banana factory in Mpraeso and its expected demand for bananas from Jaikasa, Ntomem, and other nearby communities. Agricultural development can connect smallholder farmers with larger-scale processing and create new economic opportunities.

8. J. B. Danquah and Bepong

Bepong is associated with the birth of J. B. Danquah. The local historical account connects his birth in Bepong to his father's movement from traditional spiritual service at Ahenfie Palace towards the Christian missionary movement. Use this story to discuss the transition from traditional spiritual systems to Christianity.

9–13. Asakraka: Cemetery, Timber, Teak and Bamboo

As you approach the outskirts of Asakraka, point out the cemetery as a boundary marker signalling the end of Bepong and the transition towards Asakraka.

Bepong and the surrounding Kwahu area have historically been associated with significant timber resources. Nana Wiafe Kwadjane established a tobacco factory known as Novotex and was associated with what was described as the largest tobacco plantation in Kwahu. The factory is no longer operating, with lack of sustainability contributing to its closure.

Teak is processed locally and exported to some extent, while bamboo is used in domestic construction, local building activities, and fish farming. Around Adawso, fish farmers use hundreds of bamboo pieces beneath the water to create places where fish can enter and breed. Other hardwoods include Odum and Emire. Discuss responsible harvesting and sustainable livelihoods.

14–15. Asakraka: Kwahu Architecture and Wealth

After Fidjasko Hotel, look towards the white house with coconut plants around the front area. Use it as a visual example of Kwahu residential architecture. After it, point out the house associated with Shaba, a board director for the Ghana Tourism Authority. It was built specifically for funeral activities.

Some wealthy people construct houses or facilities specifically for funerals. Funerals are not only mourning occasions; they can also become major social, family, and community gatherings.

16–20. Approaching Tafo

Point out the Asakraka water reservoir and use it to introduce Nkrumah's historical footprint in Obomeng, Tafo, and Nketepa. Explain that Nkonima established an entire community known as Nketepa.

At Choba, ask tourists to look carefully into the rear-view mirror area to see a distant, protruding view of Kwahu Bruku Rock. When the tour reaches the Forestry Checkpoint, visitors may see sculptures depicting past Catholic saints. Explain Catholic devotion and the meeting of African culture, Christianity, and local spiritual expression.

As the tour continues into Tafo, introduce Bruku, regarded within the traditional spiritual understanding of the area as being above smaller shrines or gods in the Kwahu spiritual hierarchy. Present this respectfully as part of Kwahu traditional belief and cultural heritage.

Tafo to Abetifi Stone Age Park

This part of the journey connects traditional leadership, Christianity, missionary history, education, Stone Age culture, traditional crafts, funerals, community entertainment, conservation, and adventure tourism.

36. Tafo Palace

After leaving Tafo, point towards the palace on the left. Ask tourists to look at the crow at the top of the building and explain that these symbols form part of the traditional identity and visual language of the community.

37–38. Community Library and the Road to Abetifi

Before the roundabout, introduce the community library and Jeabour Asante, a Kwahu personality known for comedy in the United Kingdom. He was the first to star in a comedy called Desmond in the UK and later helped establish the library with local state-owned companies as partners.

At the sharp turn, explain that the road passes through Bukruwa and Pepease towards Abetifi. The Ramsayer/Presbyterian missionary experience comes first, followed by the Stone Age Park.

39–43. Ramseyer Missionary Heritage

The Ramseyer area provides background for understanding how Christianity and formal education developed in Kwahu. Missionary activity introduced formal education and Christian worship, creating significant changes in social and educational life. Traditional religious systems and customs remained strong, so the new religion sometimes met resistance.

The missionary identified a location considered the highest habitable point in the area and established a church there. The location continues to host annual gatherings, large church programmes, meetings, worship activities, and special religious events. Ask permission before entering or walking around the premises, and keep a normal visit under 30 minutes unless tourists want more time.

44–47. Abetifi Stone Age Park, Food and God's Axe

The Stone Age Park allows tourists to explore how people lived and used natural materials. Discuss food production, processing, traditional farming, palm trees, banana fibre, animal products, and locally available materials.

Ask: “How many different things do you think one palm tree can provide for a community?” Introduce God's Axe, a story many people heard as children: that an axe fell from the sky. The park offers another interpretation, showing tools deliberately made and processed by human beings.

48–50. Banana Fibre, Animal Skin and Events

Banana fibre could be processed and used around the body of a deceased person during the period between death and burial. Burial arrangements could take seven days or longer, allowing the family time to investigate the circumstances of death and complete family and traditional arrangements. Present this respectfully as a traditional practice and historical belief.

Animal skin could be treated and used for traditional crafts, household uses, cultural practices, and traditional objects. The park can accommodate approximately 2,000 people for funerals, weddings, entertainment, camping, community gatherings, and other large events. Point out the large bonfire stand.

51–55. Views, Conservation and Future Plans

Point out Bruku Rock in the distance and the wider view towards the Afram Plains. The park also has a children's playground, although local patronage has been relatively limited because the entrance/play cost is approximately ₵30 per child.

Introduce the conservation vision of the founder, Mr. Ben Addo. He remembers vegetation so dense that he could hardly see the sky. The park aims to preserve part of the environment so future generations can experience something closer to the landscape of the past.

The park has plans for aerial cable transportation or cable-taxi experiences. Present this as a future development concept, rather than an existing attraction.

Natural resource → Human innovation → Human life → Modern use → Natural heritage → Future.
Mpraeso to Abetifi

This section introduces tourists to clan identity, education, hospitality, traditional justice, local leadership, philanthropy, and the development of Abetifi as the seat of Kwahu traditional government.

1–2. Mpraeso: The Asona Clan and Secondary School

Empraeso belongs to the Asona Clan. Clan identity is an important part of understanding traditional Kwahu society. The Asona people are traditionally associated with statesmanship and leadership. Present this as a cultural association, rather than suggesting that a person's ability to lead is determined solely by birth.

Point out Mpraeso Secondary School and connect it to Julius Debrah, Chief of Staff, where appropriate, or other notable individuals associated with the institution. Education has contributed to the development of Kwahu's people and has produced individuals who serve in important positions in Ghana and beyond.

3–4. Rock City and Kwahu Palace

After passing the cemetery, ask tourists to look left towards the large Rock City development. It is a major hospitality and tourism development associated with hotel accommodation, large conferences, entertainment, hospitality, large gatherings, and MICE tourism: Meetings, Incentives, Conferences and Exhibitions.

Explain the planned scale, including a 1,000-capacity conference hall and approximately 1,000 rooms, with a longer-term target of about 2,000 rooms. As you continue towards Abetifi, point out the Kwahu palace on the right and give tourists time to observe the printed information about the Asona Clan and the crow.

5–10. Aduamoa, the First Fort and Traditional Court

Aduamoa is associated with the famous local nut known as Atadwe. The community is also associated locally with what is described as the first fort in the Kwahu area, which had functions including keeping prisoners and carrying out significant traditional activities.

Within the palace area, point out the traditional court and public court area. Explain that matters could first be presented publicly before being considered in the traditional court.

Point out the three trees and their lessons: Nyame Dua represents the presence of God; Asawa teaches that even when speaking about something bitter, words should be pleasant and respectful; Odum teaches people to think carefully before speaking. Together: remember God, speak with sweetness and respect, and think before you speak.

11–13. The Chief, Snails and the Wadie Family

Traditionally, the chief did not necessarily have to go to the farm to understand whether there had been a good harvest. The presence of many snails could be interpreted as an indication of a good harvest. Present this as traditional knowledge and belief.

As you approach Abetifi Police Station, point out the large houses belonging to the Wadie family. The family is prominent and wealthy, and is associated with a donated courthouse/court building, hospital rehabilitation, road development in Abetifi, other community initiatives, and Freedom House. Use this to explain philanthropy and community development.

14–18. Entering Abetifi

A commonly told explanation connects Abetifi with “Abɛ no etifi”, meaning “At the top of the palm plant”, relating to the town's elevated position. Abetifi is known for having the highest habitable point in the area.

Abetifi is an educational centre with Abetifi Training College, university-level education, and Avu Technical Institute. Earlier we spoke about missionaries introducing formal education; today, Abetifi continues that educational tradition.

Explain that Abetifi serves as an important centre of traditional government in Kwahu and is the seat of Kwahu traditional administration. When discussing modern development, introduce Brian Acheampong, the Member of Parliament associated with the constituency, and his connection to Rock City Hotel.

Kwahu's identity has been built through clans, chiefs, education, traditional justice, agriculture, business, philanthropy, and modern development.
Bruku Hike

Bruku Hike is an adventure and cultural experience built around steep climbs, rocky pathways, and spectacular summit views.

Along the trail, share local folklore associated with the mountain and traditional beliefs. Explain Bruku respectfully as part of the traditional spiritual understanding of the area, while allowing visitors to appreciate the landscape from their own perspective.

The hike offers one of the best panoramic viewpoints in the municipality. Brief visitors about the terrain, footwear, pace, hydration, and safety before beginning, and allow time at the summit for photography and quiet appreciation of the Kwahu landscape.

Odweanoma Route

Odweanoma is one of the prominent mountains in the Eastern Region, rising to approximately 2,500 feet above sea level. The mountain is important to adventure tourism because of its paragliding take-off field.

20. Odweanoma Mountain

Point out the mountain and explain its elevation and importance to the Eastern Region landscape. Use the mountain as an introduction to the way adventure, nature, culture, geology, communication, and community life meet in Kwahu.

21. Paragliding Take-Off Field

Point out the paragliding take-off field. Explain that the site became associated with organised paragliding following its discovery and promotion by Jake Obetsebi-Lamptey, who served as Minister of Tourism during the John Agyekum Kufuor administration around 2005.

Initially, only one person was able to fly from the take-off field. Today, the site has developed to the point where three tandem pilots can operate flights. You can also introduce Jonathan Quaye, identified as a Ghanaian tandem pilot associated with the country's paragliding activities.

22. Odweanoma and Communication Masts

As you move around the mountain, tourists will notice numerous telecommunication and broadcasting masts. Explain that the mountain's elevation makes it useful for companies and broadcasters that need to place infrastructure at high points to improve coverage across the surrounding terrain.

23. Odjianoma Shrines

There are also traditional shrines on the mountain. Explain that the mountain is not only an adventure destination; it also forms part of the traditional spiritual landscape of Kwahu. Visitors should approach these places respectfully and should only enter or participate in any traditional activity with appropriate permission.

24. Odweanoma Swing

A swing attraction has also been constructed on the mountain. At the time of the tour, explain its status accurately, particularly if it has not yet entered active public use. Present it as part of the mountain's developing adventure-tourism infrastructure.

25. The Odweanoma Mountain Trail

For visitors interested in hiking, explain that there is also a mountain trail on Odjianoma. The trail can lead towards the Pillars of Odweanoma, large rock pillars and formations that create an impressive geological feature on the mountain.

Use this section to discuss the natural formation of the landscape and the way the rocks appear to support and frame the mountain.

26. Sunset Experience

One of the best times to experience Odjianoma is around sunset. From the mountain, tourists can enjoy wide views across the surrounding landscape. On a clear evening, point out Prea Mountain, the direction towards Obo, the Trinidad Road, Bonu community, and the wider landscape stretching towards Kumasi.

“At sunset, Odweanoma becomes more than a mountain. It becomes a viewpoint from which you can read the geography of Kwahu and the surrounding communities.”

Odweanoma Experience Summary

Mountain: 2,500 ft and the Eastern Region landscape.

Adventure: Paragliding, tandem flights, hiking, the mountain trail, and the swing.

Geology: The Pillars of Odweanoma and rock formations.

Spirituality: Traditional shrines and the sacred mountain landscape.

Technology: Telecommunication and broadcasting masts.

Sunset: Prayer Mountain, Obo, and the Kumasi direction.

Guide's Golden Rule for This Route

When guiding Odjianoma, do not allow the experience to become only about taking photographs from the top. Let tourists understand the mountain through five lenses:

Adventure: What can we do here?

Nature: What does the mountain and surrounding landscape show us?

Culture: What does the mountain mean to local people?

Geology: What are the rocks and formations telling us?

Community: How does this mountain support tourism, communications, and local livelihoods?

That is what turns Odjianoma from a viewpoint into a complete Kwahu experience.

How to Present the Route

The guide should not deliver the entire narration as a memorized speech. Instead, learn the story behind each location and speak naturally.

1. Point

Tell visitors what they are looking at.

2. Explain

Give the historical, cultural, economic, or environmental context.

3. Connect

Explain how the location relates to the wider Kwahu story.

4. Engage

Ask the tourist a simple question or invite them to observe something.

“Have you noticed how many large houses we have passed since entering Kwahu? Let me tell you why this is actually an important part of Kwahu's history.”

The Bigger Story of the Mpraeso–Twenedurase Route

The route should ultimately communicate one central idea: Kwahu is more than mountains and Easter celebrations.

It is a place where traditional leadership, Christianity, entrepreneurship, agriculture, migration, architecture, government, community life, and natural heritage have developed together over generations.

By the time a visitor reaches Twenedurase and begins exploring attractions such as Nkofieho Cave of Life, the tourist should already have a deeper understanding of the people and communities surrounding the attraction.

That is the difference between simply taking tourists around Kwahu and interpreting Kwahu for tourists. A guide from LTK Tours should aim to make every kilometre of the journey meaningful. The destination begins before the attraction.

Continuing the Narration Near Nyakwamba Nyako Hotel

When you slow down near Nyakwamba Nyako Hotel, point out Governor's Close on the left, then the main building. Use this moment to tell the broader story of Kwahu as a community of entrepreneurs and professionals who often work elsewhere but invest back home.

Explain that these homes symbolize migration, hard work, and the strong connection Kwahu people maintain with their communities.

Homestay/Hotel to Attractions

After picking tourists up from the station and taking them to their homestay or hotel, help them settle in before beginning the first sightseeing experience. Most first days are best used for getting to know the community through scenic, historic, and welcoming activities.

Arrival from the Station

Welcome the tourists warmly, help with luggage where possible, and confirm that they are comfortable at the homestay or hotel. Give them a simple overview of the day, including the expected travel time, meal arrangements, walking requirements, and the order of the activities.

Use this first interaction to introduce Kwahu as a living community rather than only a collection of attractions. Point out the landscape, local homes, roads, businesses, and everyday activity as you travel from the station.

First-Day Sightseeing

Most first days are for sightseeing and orientation. Begin with places that help visitors understand the scenery, history, and character of the community before moving into more demanding activities.

Stone Age Scenic and Historic Sites

Take tourists to the Stone Age Park or another suitable scenic and historic site. Explain how the park connects natural materials, traditional food, farming, tools, crafts, burial customs, community events, and conservation. Encourage visitors to look at the landscape while listening to the story of how people used what nature provided.

Ramseyer Site

Include the Ramseyer site when time, access, and the visitors' interests allow. Use the site to introduce missionary history, Christianity, formal education, the highest habitable point, and the way traditional beliefs and new religious institutions interacted in Kwahu. Ask permission before entering church premises or walking around restricted areas.

Trash from Arts

Where this activity is available, introduce tourists to Trash from Arts as an example of community creativity and the value of reusing materials. Connect the activity to local skills, environmental responsibility, art, and the wider idea that tourism can support people who create meaningful products and experiences from resources around them.

Sunset Watch

End the day with a sunset watch at a suitable viewpoint, such as Odweanoma or another approved scenic location. Point out the changing light across the mountains, valleys, and surrounding communities. Use the quiet moment to help visitors reflect on what they have seen and to connect the landscape with the people and history of Kwahu.

“Your first day is not only about ticking attractions off a list. It is about helping you arrive in the community, understand the landscape, and begin to feel the story of Kwahu.”

Other Community Activities

Depending on the season, opening hours, weather, access, and visitor interests, include other activities that relate to coming into the community. These may include a local market or town walk, a pottery or craft experience, a coffee or agricultural visit, a cultural site, a short viewpoint stop, or a conversation about local architecture and community life.

Always confirm availability and permission before promising an activity. Keep the pace comfortable, leave room for questions and photographs, and adapt the programme to the tourists' arrival time and energy level.

Guide's Connection

The station-to-homestay journey is the beginning of the visitor's relationship with Kwahu. By combining hospitality, scenic views, historic sites, creative community activities, and sunset, the guide helps tourists understand that the destination begins before the main attraction.

Location

Where Kwahu Sits in Ghana

Kwahu is located in the Eastern Region along the Nkawkaw–Konongo highway, occupying the highest section of the Kwahu Plateau — approximately 450 to 800 metres above sea level.

It sits almost midway between Accra and Kumasi, making it one of Ghana's most accessible destinations for both domestic and international visitors.

Kwahu Knowledge

👑 Kwahu People & Clans

Understanding who the Kwahu people are — their origin, values, and governance — is essential for any guide who wants to give tourists a deeper experience.

Identity

Who Are the Kwahu People?

The Kwahu are an Akan subgroup who migrated from Bono and Ashanti territories centuries ago and settled on the Kwahu Plateau. They speak Twi (Kwahu dialect) and practice a matrilineal system — family lineage passes through the mother.

Traditional Leadership

Kwahu Paramountcy

  • The Kwahu Traditional Area is headed by the Kwahumanhene (Paramount Chief)
  • The paramount stool is located in Abene — the traditional capital
  • The current paramountcy belongs to the Bretuo Clan
  • Each town has its own divisional chief, all recognising the Kwahumanhene's authority
  • The Kwahumanhene preserves customary law, settles disputes, and promotes development
Clan System

The 8 Akan Clans (Abusua)

Each Kwahu person belongs to one of eight principal clans. Clan membership is inherited through the mother and connects people across all Akan communities in Ghana.

Kwahu Knowledge

🥁 Culture & Daily Life

Set expectations for visitors before they arrive. A prepared tourist is a happy tourist.

Traditions

Culture

Kwahu is deeply rooted in Akan traditions — respect for elders, traditional leadership, family systems, and cultural heritage all remain central to daily life.

Visitors may witness naming ceremonies, funerals, church activities, festivals, and chieftaincy events. Encourage guests to observe respectfully and ask permission before photographing.

People

The Kwahu People

Known across Ghana for being hardworking, entrepreneurial, hospitable, and community-oriented. Many operate businesses across the country but maintain strong ties to their hometowns.

English and Twi are both widely spoken — international tourists will find communication easy.

Architecture

Big Buildings & Modern Homes

Many visitors are surprised by the impressive homes throughout Kwahu. Explain: these are built by successful entrepreneurs and diaspora Kwahus investing in their ancestral communities.

During Easter, these homes become family gathering places. The buildings represent both personal success and deep community attachment.

Animals

Household Animals

Expect chickens, goats, sheep, ducks, guinea fowls, and occasionally cattle moving freely — especially in rural areas. This is normal community life.

Advise visitors to drive cautiously through villages and avoid feeding animals without the owner's permission.

Sound & Life

Music & Community Life

Kwahu communities are lively — gospel music from churches, traditional drumming during ceremonies, contemporary Ghanaian music from shops and homes.

During Easter, the atmosphere is electric: concerts, street celebrations, and cultural performances draw visitors from across Ghana and the diaspora.

Attractions

⛰ All Attractions

Know every attraction deeply. Visitors may forget names — but they never forget the story you told them.

🕳

Nkofieho Cave (Cave of Life)

Adventure · Cultural

Narrow tunnels, underground chambers, and ancient rock formations in Twenedurase. Visitors crawl, climb, and navigate through confined spaces. Deep cultural significance — the cave symbolises courage, survival, and hope. Wish-making is part of the experience.

🎙

Guides play an essential safety role here. Always brief visitors on what to expect physically before entering.

💧

Butuase Waterfalls

Nature · Eco-Tourism

Hidden within lush forest. Short forest walk leads to the falls — flowing most of the year, strongest in rainy season. Visitors relax, photograph, and experience rich biodiversity.

🌿

Talk about the ecological role of Kwahu's forests as watersheds supporting both wildlife and nearby communities.

⛰

Bruku God Hiking

Adventure · Culture

Steep climbs, rocky pathways, and spectacular summit views. Along the trail, share local folklore associated with the mountain and traditional beliefs. One of the best panoramic viewpoints in the municipality.

👁

Hidden View (Prayer Mountain)

Viewpoint · Spiritual

A peaceful location used for personal reflection, prayer, and meditation. Beyond religious significance, it offers spectacular hidden viewpoints over valleys, forests, and distant communities. Cool mountain air and quiet surroundings.

🙏

Respect the spiritual nature of this site. Speak quietly and guide visitors to observe the environment thoughtfully.

🔊

Echo Ravine

Viewpoint · Adventure

Visitors clap or call out and hear their voices return from the valley. Combines science (sound reflection from rock faces and valley shape) with adventure and stunning mountain scenery. Excellent photography opportunities.

🗿

Stone Age Park

Cultural · Adventure

Enormous natural rock formations believed to be millions of years old. Narrow rock corridors, climbing viewpoints, and dramatic landscapes. Great for photography, family visits, and educational tours. Discuss Ghana's geological history and weathering processes.

🌄

Highest Habitable Point (Ramseyer Site)

Viewpoint · History

Breathtaking panoramic views over the Kwahu Escarpment, Afram Plains, and surrounding valleys. Named after Basel missionary Fritz Ramseyer, whose work contributed to Christianity and education in Kwahu. Popular for photography and sunrises/sunsets.

🏺

Mpraeso Pottery Experience

Cultural · Interactive

Local women practising hand-built pottery techniques passed down through generations. Visitors can mould their own clay pots under expert guidance — interactive, not just a demonstration. Supports local livelihoods while preserving cultural heritage.

📖

Proverbs from Earth

Cultural · Storytelling

Natural rock formations used to teach traditional Akan wisdom. Each location carries a proverb on leadership, unity, perseverance, family, or respect for nature. Visitors engage in storytelling and discussion — tourism meets indigenous knowledge.

🏊

Freedom Waterfalls

Nature · Wellness

An emerging eco-tourism site where visitors can swim in clean natural pools within unspoiled forest. Promotes relaxation, wellness, and appreciation of Ghana's natural resources.

Safety Note: Always assess water conditions and prioritise visitor safety before allowing swimming.
🧗

Asubone Climbing Festival

Adventure · Community

A community sporting event centred on climbing massive granite boulders. Originally a traditional show of bravery, now an adventure tourism festival. Highlights Kwahu's potential as a rock climbing destination.

🚣

Afram Lake Canoeing (Mother Trees)

Nature · Eco-Tourism

Guided canoe rides on the Afram Basin. Visit the historic "Mother Trees" — enormous indigenous trees symbolising strength and continuity. Combines birdwatching, storytelling, fishing traditions, and environmental education.

🌙

Nightlife in Kwahu South

Cultural · Entertainment

Restaurants, lounges, pubs, music venues, and cultural entertainment. During Easter, Kwahu South becomes one of Ghana's busiest entertainment destinations. Outside Easter, relaxed evening experiences with local hospitality remain available year-round.
